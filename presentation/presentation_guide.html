2. The 3D Globe & Satellite Interaction

The core visual centerpiece of the dashboard is the interactive 3D Globe. Here is a guide to its mechanics:

Slide 2: Interactive 3D Globe Mapping and Satellite Telemetry Readouts

A. What do the colored dots represent?

  • Blue Dots = Starlink satellites (spacex constellation).
  • Yellow Dots = OneWeb satellites (communications constellation).
  • Purple/Violet Dots = Iridium satellites (legacy network).

B. What do the rings and lines represent?

  • Orbital Rings: The thin colored trails around the Earth represent the past and future orbital tracks calculated for each satellite.
  • Telescope Cones (Green Cones): Glowing light pyramids pointing outward from the Earth. These are the fields of view of the ground observatories in India (Hanle and Devasthal), projecting where the telescopes are pointed.
  • Intersection Path: When a satellite is about to cross a telescope cone, its path turns solid red, indicating a conflict.

C. What happens when you click a satellite dot?

  1. The camera centers and locks onto the satellite's path, rotating with it.
  2. The satellite's trail glows solid red on the globe.
  3. The right-hand panel slides open to show Satellite Telemetry, drawing real calculations:
    • Altitude: Actual distance in kilometers above Earth.
    • Inclination: Angle relative to the equator.
    • Period: Minutes required for one complete orbit.
    • Velocity: Computed using Keplerian mechanics (~7.5 km/s).
    • Interference Probability: Likelihood of blocking a target over the next 24 hours.

3. Dashboard Controls (Button-by-Button Guide)

Every interactive button on the Mission Control dashboard is documented below:

Button / Control Location What It Does (Functionality)
Logo Header Top Left Returns the user to the landing page.
LIVE Button Top Center Resets the simulator time back to the current real-world UTC time.
+1h, +6h, +1d, +1w Top Center Skips the simulation clock forward. The satellites instantly jump to their calculated mathematical positions for that future date.
Play/Pause (▶ / ❚❚) Top Center Pauses or resumes the continuous orbit calculations.
Speed Multipliers (1x - 1000x) Top Center Changes how fast time flows. 1000x makes satellites fly around the globe, showing orbital paths clearly.
Density (HIGH / MED) Top Right Toggles rendering load. "MED" hides half of the satellites to ensure smooth rendering on slower computers.
RUN DEMO Button Top Right Triggers the automated threat demonstration scenario.
Observation Rows Left Panel Clicking a row highlights that celestial target (such as an asteroid or transit exoplanet) and changes the right panel to show target coordinates.
Close button (✕) Right Panel Closes the telemetry details card and returns to default dashboard state.

4. Conjunction & Resolution Logic

The platform models intersections by evaluating telescope fields-of-view against satellite trajectories. The diagram below illustrates the conjunction hazard and the shifted window calculation:

Slide 3: Visualizing Conjunction Dangers (Top) and AI Optimization Resolution (Bottom)

Under-the-Hood Math (For Technical Q&A):

  • SGP4 Orbit Propagator: Recalculates satellite ECI coordinates every time step using real Keplerian formulas.
  • FOV Intersection Cones: Telescope pointing angles (Right Ascension and Declination) are converted to pointing vectors. The system checks if the angle between the pointing vector and the satellite range vector is less than 1.5 degrees.
  • Horizon Constraint: Prevents false alarms by checking if the satellite is above the horizon (>10° elevation) from the telescope’s coordinate location.
  • Priority-Based Scheduler: Ensures that resolving a low-tier conflict doesn't block or displace a higher-priority target.
